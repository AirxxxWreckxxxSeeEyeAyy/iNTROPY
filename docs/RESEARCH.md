# Research and evidence standards

## Purpose

Support credible equipment and load-shifting comparisons, renewable integration, and customer comprehension. This document defines a review process; it does not certify the current model or assert that proposed research has already been implemented.

## Evidence hierarchy and citation record

Use authoritative agency datasets for environmental inputs; peer-reviewed original research and validated technical methods for engineering claims; and equipment-specific documentation for product specifications. A manufacturer claim is not independent validation.

Record each claim, original source URL or DOI, author/organization, publication date, peer-review status, geography, equipment assumptions, applicable model or interface feature, limitations, and review outcome. Distinguish papers merely identified for review from papers reviewed in full.

Google Scholar is a discovery service covering several publication types, including preprints and theses. Appearance in its results is not proof of peer review. [Google Scholar help](https://scholar.google.com/intl/us/scholar/help.html)

## Application data and modeling references

- [NASA POWER methodology](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/): solar-resource context. Keep climatology, forecast, and measured time series distinct.
- [NOAA climate normals](https://www.noaa.gov/explainers/understanding-climate-normals): historical climate context; normals describe a reference period rather than current conditions.
- [Sandia PVPMC isotropic sky diffuse model](https://pvpmc.sandia.gov/modeling-guide/1-weather-design-inputs/plane-of-array-poa-irradiance/calculating-poa-irradiance/poa-sky-diffuse/isotropic-sky-diffuse-model/): reference for the simplified diffuse-irradiance calculation and its assumptions.
- [NREL/DOE City and County Energy Profiles](https://data.openei.org/submissions/149): historical modeled county data. The application documentation records the Michael Minn redistribution and transformations.
- NOAA UV bulletins and GOES imagery provide separate environmental context; neither is a substitute for quantitative rooftop solar measurements.

These sources support specific inputs or methods, not endorsement of the application.

## Literature-review starting points

The following are candidates for detailed review, not claims that their methods or results validate iNTROPY:

- [REopt residential solar-plus case study and associated Applied Energy article](https://www.nrel.gov/reopt/projects/case-study-residential-storage.html): a starting point for research on PV, battery storage, and dispatchable residential loads.
- [Simple interventions can correct misperceptions of home energy use, Nature Energy (2019)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-019-0467-2): a starting point for research on customer understanding of energy use.
- [DOE fuel cells](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/fuels/fuel-cells): background on fuel-to-electricity conversion for the version-3 research stream.
- [DOE Energy Storage Grand Challenge Roadmap](https://www.energy.gov/documents/energy-storage-grand-challenge-roadmappdf): background on storage technologies, including flywheels.

Complete paper-level review before translating findings into product claims.

## Public-university research stream

Seek relevant peer-reviewed work from public STEM and engineering universities, including a targeted stream from the top 10 public institutions in a specifically identified U.S. News & World Report engineering ranking.

Before naming a school as top 10, record the edition/year, exact category (such as undergraduate or graduate engineering), whether the ranking is public-only or filtered from an overall list, rank/ties, and authoritative supporting link. Do not substitute a general national-university ranking for an engineering ranking.

No top-10 roster has been verified for this project. Selection remains pending that category/edition check. Judge individual papers by methods, relevance, and reproducibility; affiliation and ranking alone do not establish engineering validity. Include strong relevant evidence beyond the ranked group.

## Customer comprehension and attention review

Planned evaluation tasks should check whether customers can:

- distinguish kW from kWh and storage from generation;
- explain how reserve and efficiency affect usable battery energy;
- identify whether a displayed value is measured, forecast, historical, or modeled;
- compare equipment scenarios and explain their assumptions;
- distinguish daily modeled energy savings from whole-life cost or payback.

Measure task success, misunderstandings, error rates, and time to complete defined tasks. Clicks, hover time, and interaction heatmaps alone do not establish attention, understanding, or informed choice. Record consent and minimize personal data in usability studies.

## Credentialing and claims

Before claiming professional review, document the reviewer's relevant credentials, consent to attribution, review scope, date, and unresolved findings. Code review, usability testing, paper citations, professional engineering review, and equipment certification are distinct activities.

Do not claim NASA, NOAA, Google, U.S. News, or university endorsement. Do not publish certification badges or “peer-reviewed software” claims without evidence of that specific assessment.
